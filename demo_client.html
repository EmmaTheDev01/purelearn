Mathematics Notes

In calculus, the derivative of a function measures the instantaneous rate of change. Formally, it is defined as the limit of the average rate of change as the time interval shrinks to zero:

$$\frac{dy}{dx} = \lim_{\Delta x \to 0} \frac{f(x + \Delta x) - f(x)}{\Delta x}$$

When dealing with trigonometric functions like the sine wave that models our alternating electric signal, something remarkable happens. The derivative of the sine function is the cosine function:

$$\frac{d}{dx} (\sin(x)) = \cos(x)$$

• What does $\sin(x)$ represent? Your vertical position on the wave.

• What does $\cos(x)$ represent? Your instantaneous vertical velocity (how fast your height is changing).

2. Worked Example: Analyzing a Periodic Wave

Consider an oscillating charge function $q(t) = Q_0 \cos(\omega t)$. The instantaneous electric current is the time rate of change of charge:

$$I(t) = \frac{dq}{dt} = -Q_0 \omega \sin(\omega t)$$
